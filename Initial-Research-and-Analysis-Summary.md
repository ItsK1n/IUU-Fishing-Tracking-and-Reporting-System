**Approach to Gathering Information**
Our research uses mixed-methods approach, encompassing both qualitative and quantitative techniques.

- Reviewing interviews with stakeholders gaining better understanding into their goals and challenges related to combating IUU fishing
- Analyzing relevant articles on IUU fishing to gain additional insights and information
- Performing user interface analysis to understand how current technology addresses IUU fishing

**Initial Findings:**
- Interviews with stakeholders provided valuable insights into their goals and challenges in order to combat IUU
- Satellite imagery is valuable in complementing AIS signals to fill information gaps but has limitations including cloud obstruction and time-of-day constraints
- IUU practices are common in "Northwest Pacific and areas adjacent to the Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZs) of Argentina and West African nations"

**Key Takeaways:**
- Annually, IUU fishing results in the loss of over 650,000 marine lives
- Globally, IUU fishing contributes to estimated annual losses of up to $36.4 billion. 
- 6% of fishing vessels disables AIS devices, researchers found that over 55,000 vessels intentional disabling AIS and obscuring nearly 5 million hours of fishing vessel activity

**Application of Findings:**
- Addressing AIS Disabling: Send alerts when AIS device is disabled, using satellite to track and capture last known location. If within view, continue taking pictures every minute. 
- Focus on High-Risk Areas: Allocate resources and attention to high-risk regions and provide alerts when vessels enter these areas
- Raise Public Awareness: Apply key findings to highlight IUU fishing's significant impacts. Develop initiatives promoting responsible fishing and marine conservation
- Enhance Monitoring: Utilize Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) to capture vessel location, distance, speed, and direction
