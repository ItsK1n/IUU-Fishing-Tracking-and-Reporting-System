Illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing is a major threat to the depletion of marine resources, with more than 650,000 marine life dying yearly as a result of illegal fishing worldwide. The challenge at hand is to develop a system capable of utilizing satellite imagery and geospatial data to effectively monitor IUU fishing activities, which is crucial for the facilitation of timely prosecutions and the sustainability of marine ecosystems.

**Impact**
- **Ecological:** IUU fishing disrupts marine biodiversity and leads to the overexploitation of fish stocks, threatening the health of ocean ecosystems. IUU fishing accounts for up to 26 million tonnes of fish caught annually, representing up to 20% of the total global catch.
- **Economic:** Global economies suffer as IUU fishing undermines legal fishing industries and results in significant losses for governments and legitimate businesses. This illegal activity results in an estimated economic loss of between $10 billion and $23 billion each year.
- **Social and Food Security**: Ocean supports the livelihoods of approximately 520 million people engaged in fishing and related activities, with 2.6 billion people depending on fish as a vital part of their diet. IUU fishing threatens these food sources and the economic stability of communities reliant on fishing.

The issue is particularly prevalent in regions with limited monitoring resources, such as international waters. The challenge intensifies during peak fishing seasons, breeding times, and holidays. The current systems are insufficient, often failing to provide timely and precise data needed to effectively reduce IUU fishing activities. 

**Stakeholders Impacted**
- Government and Regulatory bodies who require precise and timely data to enforce laws and prosecute violations effectively
- Commercial Fishing Enterprises and Sustainability Officers who need reliable systems to ensure compliance with regulations and sustainably manage fishing practices
- Local and global communities who depend on fishing stocks for their dietary needs and economic security
- Data analysts who rely on robust data infrastructure for accurate analysis that supports regulatory and legal processes
- Environmental and conservation organizations who dedicated to protecting marine biodiversity and promoting sustainable use of marine resources

**The Need for Action**
Each year, illegal, unreported, and unregulated fishing reduces global fish stocks, threatening biodiversity and undermining the economic stability of communities reliant on marine resources.
Urgent action is required to address IUU fishing to mitigate its detrimental effects and ensure the preservation of marine ecosystems.
