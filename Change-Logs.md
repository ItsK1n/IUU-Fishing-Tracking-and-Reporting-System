<h1> Changes 1.0 </h1>

No changes were made to the Current State, Stakeholders, and Research Summary pages, but we did additional research, and below are some of the interesting things we found:

- Existing AIS tracking system has many flaws from "faulty AIS devices, user error, intentional manipulation, crowded areas, poor satellite reception, and transmission flaws are factors that contribute to noise and errors in AIS data, and sometimes those inaccuracies can be reflected in the location of a vessel. Vessel operators can accidentally or purposefully enter false information into their ship’s AIS, thus concealing their identity or location." 
- The U.S. only requires large boats (longer than 65 feet) to have AIS which accounts for only 12.4% of the U.S.’s fishing fleet. Additionally AIS has to be turned on within 12 nautical miles of shore, not only does this cover around 8% of country’s waters but this doesn't account for high waters in which is known for the most occurrence of IUU fishing.
- Satellite real-time imagery could take between minutes to capture data and delivery it up to a few days if we want a high resolution imagery.
- Satellite faces challenges when encountering clouds, with oceans being cloudier than land, the average total cloud covers can range from of 54% to 68% of the ocean
- Vessel Monitoring System (VMS) is designed for fisheries management and monitoring, which provides more detailed information about vessel activities, including fishing catch traceability, vessel identification, vessel movement, and compliance with fishing regulations
- VMS requires those on the fishing vessels to send reports periodically in intervals ranging from 10 mins to 1 hour 
- VMS requirements for a vessel varies depending on regions
- Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) emit an electromagnetic signal which  can then capture vessel location, distance, speed, and direction and is not limited by clouds
